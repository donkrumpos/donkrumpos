For twenty-five years I built websites and systems for other people's companies. Corporate design systems, agency war rooms, apps used by millions, the polished work that pays well and photographs nicely.

The whole time I was painting murals on the side, and paying more attention to the marsh hawks and the moods of Lake Michigan than to anybody's quarterly roadmap.

Here's what took me too long to see. The skills I sharpened in those corporate and agency years are exactly the ones small organizations can never afford and need the most. A community nonprofit, a corner shop, a family business doing honest work. They get handed a template and a monthly bill and told to be grateful, while the good tools, the fast and durable kind big companies take for granted, stay locked behind budgets they'll never have.

We have somehow built an entire industry that rents people back their own front door. I don't think it has to be that way, and I've spent the last few years quietly proving it doesn't. So I brought the practice home, set up a workbench on the far shore, and started building for the people the industry overcharges and overlooks. Made by hand, built to last, made to fit, and yours to keep.

I'm going to show that work here. The real projects, how they were made, and what they do that a template can't. Not a pitch. Just the work, laid out plain, from someone who would rather build you something you own than rent you something you don't.

Same hands that paint the walls. Turns out they're good for this too.
